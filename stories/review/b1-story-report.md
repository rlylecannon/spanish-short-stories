# B1 stories review (b1-01 … b1-10)

Files: `b1-stories-fixed.json` (10 stories, all fields), `b1-story-changes.json` (117 changes, each with a reason), generator `_apply_b1.py`.
Checks: both files load as JSON. `validate.py … B1 1` passes. Line counts and line order are unchanged, `<b>` tags are balanced, and no titles, plots or characters were changed.
Ratings are for the stories **as submitted**, out of 5. Average: **3.9**. After the fixes I would put every story at 4 or above.

## Per-story assessment

**b1-01 La cartera verde: 4/5.** A clean, warm, well-paced opener. The preterite/imperfect split is textbook (scene in the imperfect, events in the preterite) and the connectors are well signposted. Weaknesses: *enseñó el barrio* happened while they were sitting in a café, and the English said "told him about". I added *Después* and fixed the English. The future-in-the-past appears only twice (estaría, mandaría/haría). All five quiz items were recall or inference with no vocab item, so the easiest one became a question on *no hace falta* / *hijo*. Two vocab entries were never bolded, and *temblar* was glossed while the text uses *temblorosas*.

**b1-02 Los jueves de mate: 3/5.** Charming and authentic: mate as a sign of trust, *¡qué bárbaro!*, the rehearsal montage. But the declared target, the future tense, barely appears: only *seré* and *enseñaré*, and the concepts cited *podré* and *iré*, which aren't in the text. I added *harás* and *gustará* in natural spots (lines 8 and 13). Other problems: *sus primeras citas* implied romantic dates, the Rioplatense words (*qué bárbaro*, *mate*) weren't glossed, and *dar vergüenza* was mis-glossed. The prompt asked for the conditional instead of the future. Montevideo speakers would mix in voseo (*tenés, querés*). The story stays with tú, which is defensible for a learner text, but a note on the site would help.

**b1-03 El horno de doña Remedios: 4/5.** It has the strongest community theme and good present perfect in Mexican usage (*se ha agrietado, me han quitado, lo hemos hablado*), which the concepts never mentioned. The *si* + imperfect subjunctive and conditional are well placed. Two plot slips: the neighbours collect money "para el horno nuevo", but the ending has the oven *reparado*. And "the roof filled with smoke" reads as a fire, so it became the chimney. *Si fuera usted* became *Si yo fuera usted*, which is the clearer model for learners. One quiz distractor ("…inmediatamente y gratis") gave the answer away, and the concepts listed *quien*, which doesn't occur.

**b1-04 El paciente de la habitación 12: 5/5.** The best story in the set. Its emotional arc is earned. The pluperfect appears 6+ times naturally, there are strong models of the subjunctive (*antes de que naciéramos*, *nada que usted pueda*) and the conditional (*si pudiera, le enseñaría*), and the ending reverses the "window" metaphor. It needed only small fixes: *revisaba suero* became *sueros*, and two bolded words had no glosses. The quiz is the most varied in the level (detail, motive, change, a quotation's meaning, outcome).

**b1-05 Dos ruedas junto al mar: 4/5.** It has rich hypotheticals (*si hubiera sido… no habríamos perdido*, *debí haber hablado*) and a good secondary character (don Walter) who mirrors the theme. Weaknesses: peninsular *enfadarse* and *echar de menos* came from Ecuadorian characters (changed to *enojarse* / *extrañar*, with the Spain forms in the glosses). Calling the house sale *una tontería* undercut the later apology, and the quiz called it a *malentendido*. The last line, *nadie pincharía la rueda del otro*, literally means sabotage and didn't match the English. The false friend *cámara* (inner tube) wasn't glossed.

**b1-06 La foto del desván: 4/5.** A satisfying mystery with excellent reported speech (*prometió que me escribiría… volvería cuando tuviera… para que mi padre lo aceptara*) and the subjunctive after *no creo que / espero que / es importante que*. Register slips in Oaxaca: *apartamento*, *no le apetecía* (both peninsular), and *mija* without a gloss. One speech in line 14 had no dialogue dashes. Quiz Q5 had an ungrammatical stem and a joke distractor ("odia a su familia"). *Desván* is more Spain than Mexico, but it's in the title, so I left it.

**b1-07 Los zapatos que hablaban: 4/5.** The funniest story, with natural Bogotá speech (*jugo*, *¿ya te tomaste…?*, *¡Ponte la bufanda, que…!*). The commands come in context (*respira, no cuentes, imaginen, observen*), with a reported command (*le dijo que se los quitara*). The concepts claimed *para que*, which never appears. One quiz option broke the sequence of tenses (*vio… acompañe*), and the prompt elicited only past narration while the targets are commands and the subjunctive. The plot detail in Q1 was the weakest question, so it became a *quedarse helada* vocab item.

**b1-08 La última tiza: 4/5.** It is moving, and the ustedes commands in Aurelio's speech (*Hagan… No tengan… devuélvanle*) model that structure very well. Errors: *se sentó en su escritorio* (= on top of the desk), *sentadas… a decenas de adultos* (agreement), and *no tienen quién* (should be *quien*, no accent). For Valparaíso, Mexican/peninsular *salón* and *pastel* became Chilean *sala* and *torta*. The English rendered *profesor* as "professor". The concepts called *no tengan* an affirmative command.

**b1-09 Una noche sin luz: 4/5.** It has the best spread of target grammar in the level: *quiero que se reúnan… que nadie salga*, *que alguien me ayude*, *cuando vuelva la luz*, *prométanme*, *ojalá repita*. Its ensemble scene suits B1 well. Costa Rican colour (*frijoles, guanábana, computadora*) is consistent, but *como si les diera pena* would read as "embarrassed" to a Central American reader, so I rewrote it. Sentences are the longest in the level (avg 16.4 words, max 39), at the top end for B1. I left them, because they are mostly lists.

**b1-10 Una visita desde lejos: 3/5.** It is the richest story in the level: the *si* + present → future pattern, *espero que abras* → *esperaba que abrieras* reported back, and a lovely final line. But the timeline was broken: twelve years of letters starting at age twelve, yet Joaquín turns thirty. Now he turns 24. There was also a tense clash (*Llevaba tanto tiempo… que casi me da*), a missing article (*de otro lado del mar*), and the calque *conocido en papel*. "Homesickness" mistranslated *añoranza*, and the dialogue punctuation at line 16 was wrong. *Rambla*, *candombe* and *campanilla* needed glosses or gloss fixes.

## Level-wide observations

1. **Level fit is good.** The stories run 439–734 words (all on target), with average sentences of 11.6–16.4 words. They show a clear progression from preterite/imperfect (01) through the pluperfect and conditional (03–05) to the subjunctive and commands (06–10). Nothing jumps beyond B1 except some long descriptive sentences in 07, 09 and 10.
2. **Regional consistency was the most common error type.** Several Latin American settings had peninsular words (*enfadarse, echar de menos, apetecer, apartamento, pastel, salón*). Regionalisms were rarely glossed (*qué bárbaro, mija, rambla, candombe*). All are now fixed or glossed. Voseo is absent from the two Montevideo stories, which is acceptable but worth one line on the site.
3. **Orthography.** The level mixed *sólo* and *solo* and used the pre-2010 *rió*. Both are normalised to *solo* and *rio* (8 line edits, all cosmetic).
4. **Quizzes** had correct keys throughout (no wrong answer found), but they leaned on detail recall. Only b1-04 and b1-06 had a meaning-in-context item. I swapped in one vocab-in-context question each in 01, 02, 03, 07, 08, 09 and 10, and fixed two ungrammatical stems plus one tense-agreement option. Answer positions are reasonably spread.
5. **Writing prompts** had no starters and often elicited a different structure from the story's target (02 conditional vs future; 07 and 09 past narration vs commands/subjunctive). All 10 now have a model starter and a grammar cue.
6. **Concepts summaries** listed forms that don't occur in four stories (02, 03, 07, 08). Those are corrected.
7. **Recycling is good for connectors** (*sin embargo, mientras tanto, al final, aunque, darse cuenta de, de pronto* recur across stories). It is weaker for content words. Glossed words like *el banco, el sobre, temblar, ahorrar, avisar* recur but are re-glossed each time instead of being treated as review.
8. **Character names repeat in ways that could confuse a series reader**, and I did not change them: **don Aurelio** is an 82-year-old astronomer in Santiago (04) *and* a retiring maths teacher in Valparaíso (08). **Inés** is a retired teacher living alone (01) and an old woman living alone (09). **Valeria** is in both 05 and 09. **Tomás** is in 01 and 06, **Lupe** in 03 and 06 (both in Oaxaca), and **Carmen** in 05 and 08. **Los Almendros** is both the hostel (01) and the building (09). Renaming the second don Aurelio is the most worthwhile follow-up.
