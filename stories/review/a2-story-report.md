## Per story

**a2-01 El tren de las nueve: 3.5, now 4.5.**
Good things: the hook (a missed train before a wedding) is strong, the turn (the broken-down train) is satisfying, and sentences are short (about 5.6 words on average). The regular preterite comes up many times, with good -ó/-ió pairs (*corrió, corrieron, subieron*).
Problems I fixed:
- The twist didn't work, because "Javier" was never introduced. He is now "Javier, mi novio".
- The quiz key called Pablo a "friend" of the groom's family. He is the groom's cousin.
- Line 11 continued Pablo's speech without a dialogue dash.
- *Ana vio su teléfono* was unnatural. It is now *miró … y vio*.
- *Su hermana tenía una boda* read as if she were a guest. It is now *Ese día era la boda de su hermana*.

Remaining weakness: *ir a* + infinitive appears only once.

**a2-02 La caja azul: 3.5, now 4.**
Good things: a lovely treasure-hunt structure, clear sequence words, natural tú commands inside the clues, and a warm ending (*Se llama Mango*).
Problems I fixed:
- A real plot hole: the key from the box was never used, and the door "opened by itself".
- *Detrás de ella … salió de la casa* didn't make sense spatially.
- *Doña* was capitalised mid-sentence.
- *Panadero* was not bolded.
- Quiz Q1 was ungrammatical.
- The concepts listed "ir" preterite, which does not appear.

Remaining weakness: the preterite verbs are varied but each occurs only once or twice, so few forms get 3 or more uses.

**a2-03 El secreto de la abuela: 4, now 4.5.**
Good things: the best emotional payoff at this level (*así mis nietos siempre me visitan*), the twist at the door is well planted, and *probar* comes up naturally 5 times. It also has a good small ser/estar contrast (*está rico / no es igual*).
Problems I fixed:

- Two quiz items had joke distractors (*No tiene lápiz*), and three of the keys were 0.

- The concepts listed *tomó* as irregular.

**a2-04 El penalti: 4.5, now 4.5.**
Good things: this is the clearest teaching of imperfect vs preterite in the set. The childhood habit (*siempre tiraba a la izquierda*) becomes the clue for the event. The imperfect comes up about 12 times, and Camila's line *mi pie decidió otra cosa* is charming.
Fixed: the twelve-year-olds remember "when we were children". This is now *pequeños*.
Not changed, but worth knowing:
- *Portero* and *penalti* are Spain-leaning words for a story set in Medellín (*arquero* and *penal* are more common in Colombia). They are kept because "El penalti" is the title.
- Strictly, the kicker's left is the keeper's right.
- The siblings share names with a2-06 (also Tomás and Camila), which may confuse readers who read the series in order.

**a2-05 Número equivocado: 4, now 4.5.**

**a2-06 La lámpara de la abuela: 4, now 4.**

Fixed: regional variety. In Chile, *bombilla* is the metal straw for mate and *apartamento* is *departamento*. Rather than rewrite the text, the vocab list now gives the Chilean variants.

**a2-07 El músico de San Telmo: 4, now 4.**
Good things: the strongest habit-vs-event frame after a2-04 (*Todas las tardes … Un jueves*), the mute girl finally speaking is a lovely payoff, and the double pronouns (*se la mostró, pagártelo*) come in context.
Fixed (previous review's flag): the text uses tú forms consistently (*ven, toca, toma*), and the grammar topic is built on those forms. So I kept tú and added the vos forms as a vocab gloss ("ven (vos: vení)"), bolded *Ven*, and updated the concepts note. I also replaced the silly distractor "comprar una moneda".
Honest caveat: a Buenos Aires grandfather would really say *vení, tocá, tomá, tocás*. If you prefer authenticity, the switch touches only lines 11, 13, 15 and 17, but the grammar topic "Tú commands: ven, toma, toca" would then need rewriting.

**a2-08 La niebla en el valle: 4, now 4.5.**

**a2-10 La noche de los faroles: 4.5, now 4.5.**

Problems I fixed:

## Level-wide observations
- **Average rating: 3.9 as submitted, 4.35 after fixes.** The stories are engaging: every one has a hook, a turn and an emotional close. Sentence length is right for A2 (5.6 to 8.8 words on average).
- **Plot logic was the most common real flaw** (a2-01, a2-02, a2-05, a2-09). These were small holes, but a careful reader trips on them, and that undermines trust in the text.
- **Regional consistency is the second weakness.** The stories are labelled by city (Medellín, Santiago, Buenos Aires, Mexico City), but the Spanish is mostly neutral or peninsular. The fixes here are glosses or minimal edits. A dedicated pass to add one or two authentic regional markers per story would add value.
- **Repetition.** Preterite and imperfect forms are plentiful, but secondary targets (*ir a*, object pronouns, present perfect) often appear only 1 or 2 times. Some vocab-list items are A1 words (*tren, mesa, calle*) that waste list slots.
- **Writing prompts** previously had no models, and two were in the present tense. All 10 now have a Spanish starter and name the target tense.
- **Quizzes** are sound overall (all answerable from the text, keys spread). I fixed 1 wrong key (a2-01), 4 weak or joke distractors, and 2 unclear stems.
