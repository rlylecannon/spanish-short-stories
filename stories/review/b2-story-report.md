# B2 stories: teacher review (b2-01 to b2-10)

Files: `b2-stories-fixed.json` (10 corrected stories, all fields), `b2-story-changes.json` (116 change records). Line counts and order are unchanged. Plots, titles and characters are unchanged. `<b>` markup is kept, and four vocab-list words that had no bold were bolded. Validator: `validate.py review/b2-stories-fixed.json B2 1` returns OK. Word counts are unchanged within ±1%.

Ratings are given as **as submitted → after fixes** (1–5).

## Per story

**b2-01 La carta sin sello (Valparaíso): 4 → 4.5**
Strengths: a model of si + imperfect subjunctive repetition (si quisiera, si pudiera ×2, si fuera ×2, si me dejara) inside a moving and economical plot. The embedded letter gives a second register, and Marta's usted vs Ignacio's tú is handled well.
Weaknesses: peninsular words in a Chilean setting (becaria, apañárselas, piso), now fixed. The English "even if" was wrong because Elvira knows Tomás is gone. The quiz had no vocab item; Q1 is now *restituido* with a "restaurado" false-friend distractor. Small plot blur that I did not change: Elvira "waited three Sundays" without ever receiving the letter.

**b2-02 El horno del patio (Mérida, Yucatán): 4 → 4.5**
Strengths: rich sensory and culinary vocabulary, and a nice ser/estar contrast (*no era terco, estaba asustado*). Good concessives (*por brillante que fuese, por mucho que*). The ending is understated and earned.
Weaknesses: *fregar los platos* is risky in Mexico, where *fregar* colloquially means "to annoy/screw up". It is now *tallar las ollas*, glossed as Mexican. The perfect *ha estado* became the Mexican preterite *estuvo*. Quiz Q5 misquoted Camila's line. *Excedencia* is fine because she works in Spain.

**b2-03 Vientos en la loma (Teruel): 4 → 4**
Strengths: excellent civic and debating register (pleno, alegatos, pliego de condiciones). Two characters move toward each other believably, and *si yo fuera usted, exigiría que se garantizaran* is a perfect B2 model.
Weaknesses: continuity error. The closed "panadería" clashed with Sergio being "el hijo del panadero"; it is now a carnicería. *La loma* and *discrepar* were in the vocab list but not bolded. Several glossed items are B1 (salvar, dividir, arruinar). The premise nearly duplicates b2-10 (see below).

**b2-04 Una frase de más (Bogotá): 4.5 → 4.5**
Strengths: the most intellectually ambitious story. It is metalinguistic (*es posible que les mintiera* vs *quizá no fui del todo sincero*) and gives English speakers a real insight into how Spanish forces a choice. It has strong pluperfect-subjunctive examples (*si hubiera querido…, lo habría escrito yo*; *ojalá yo hubiera tenido*) and the best register range of the level (letter, phone call, narration).
Weaknesses: a subject slip ("Aquel libro… se topó con una frase", which says the book bumped into a sentence) and "the publisher hoped to *deliver* the translation". Both are fixed. One detail question became a vocab item (*tachar de*).

**b2-05 Dicen que dicen (Lima): 3.5 → 4.5**
Strengths: a great engine for the target grammar. It is the densest story for pluperfect subjunctive and conditional perfect (*si hubieras hablado…, habría ocurrido; ojalá me hubiera callado; aunque hubieras permanecido…*), with reported speech in the director's speech. The irony is clear and the ending is open.
Weaknesses: peninsular corporate vocabulary in Lima (ordenador, planta, despacho, prima). These are now computadora, piso, oficina and bono, with bono glossed. **Wrong answer key:** Q4's "correct" option (rumors cost more than real layoffs) is not what the director says. It now paraphrases line 7. "Tarde comprendió" was rephrased.

**b2-06 El reloj de las cuatro y diez (Guanajuato): 3.5 → 4.5**
Strengths: a beautiful first-person narrator who misjudges the master, and an emotionally strong reveal. Subjunctive is used naturally after past requests (*me pidió que no viniera hasta que…*). Its strength is the slow build-up.
Weaknesses (most continuity problems of the level):
- He had already repaired the watch (line 6), yet she "begged me not to *fix* it". This is now "no lo pusiera en marcha".
- "Mañana empezamos con las herramientas" contradicted line 0 (no tools only in the first year, and three years have passed). This is now "empiezo a enseñarte a reparar", which echoes the opening.
- *mientras estuviera… tenía* is now *tendría*.
- The prompt referred to "Lucía", who does not exist in the story.
- The concepts listed a subjunctive relative clause that is not in the text.
- Three vocab items were not bolded, and "estropeado" was peninsular.
- The dialogue dashes had spaces after the raya.

**b2-07 Toda la verdad, aunque duela (Arequipa): 3.5 → 4.5**
Strengths: a mature ethical dilemma, with idioms well integrated (*cerrar el grifo, dar la cara, frotarse las manos*). The free indirect thought ("una verdad a medias… puede hacer más daño") is a strong B2 reading-skill target.
Weaknesses:
- Line 9 was illogical. Its "aunque" did not follow, and it said the fired accountant *revealed* the sender rather than *being* the sender. It was rewritten and keeps the cleft structure.
- "Cobrar verduras a proveedores" described the opposite of the fraud.
- *Contable* and *con la boca pequeña* are peninsular. They are now *contador* and *con la boca chica*, both glossed.
- One detail question became an idiom item.

**b2-08 La segunda parte del juramento (Valparaíso): 4 → 4.5**
Strengths: an unreliable narrator corrected by the other characters. Good cleft sentences (*fuiste tú quien se marchó*) and *quien hubiera ido más lejos*. A warm, earned reconciliation, and Chilean details (liceo, cerro, ascensor).
Weaknesses:
- The group was already at the mirador at the top of the hill but "rode up" the funicular before going to a port bar. They now ride down.
- The English "half Galician" mistranslated *gallego*, which is Latin American slang for any Spaniard. He lives in Barcelona.
- *pagaba* became *pagaría* to model future-in-the-past.
- The prompt had no target grammar or starter.

**b2-09 Sin novedad en la sala siete (Quito): 4 → 4.5**
Strengths: the most engaging plot of the level (a light mystery with a tender payoff). Clear conditionals of conjecture (*serían las tres; sería la radio*), *busco a alguien que me diga*, and *ahora bien*. A wide range of sound verbs.
Weaknesses:
- "Por segunda vez en su carrera" contradicted the setup, because he had only ever written «Sin novedad». It is now "por primera vez".
- The English "would be the radio" is now "must be".
- The prompt named no grammar target.
- Q1 was trivial and was replaced with *montar guardia* in context.

**b2-10 El sobre de Buenos Aires (Aragón): 4 → 4.5**
Strengths: the most literary prose, a strong thematic close ("la pared tuvo voz"), and conjecture with *habría votado*. It also has *fuera cual fuese*, *ocurra lo que ocurra* and *lo difícil / lo más importante*.
Weaknesses:
- Line 4 was garbled ("un rodeo y una sospecha que aquí llaman «tirar de la lengua»"). It is now "con mil rodeos, intentando… «tirarme de la lengua»".
- The English for *habría votado/elegido* rendered conjecture as a true conditional, as the previous review flagged. It is now "might have / had probably chosen".
- 142 ballots did not fit in "ciento cincuenta almas", so the village now has 200.
- The teaser misdescribed the secretary's role.
- Average sentence length is 26 words (max 55), so it reads as upper B2 / early C1. I would put it last, as it already is.
- A secretary opening a postal vote alone on a balcony is literary licence, not electoral procedure. I left it as is.

## Level-wide observations

1. **Regional leakage is the main systematic flaw.** Seven of the ten stories are set in Latin America, but the narration often defaults to peninsular Spanish: piso, ordenador, planta, despacho, prima, contable, con la boca pequeña, fregar, becaria, apañárselas, estropeado, ha estado. All instances are fixed, and the regional items are glossed with their counterpart (e.g. "bono (Lat. Am.; Spain: la prima)"). Future B2 writing should state the target variety per story.
2. **Grammar coverage is strong but narrow.** Si-clauses (imperfect and pluperfect subjunctive) appear in every story, which is good repetition, but alongside the grammar review's finding that 8 of 40 topics are si-clauses. Other structures, like reported speech, concessives, clefts, conjecture and relative + subjunctive, are well represented. Discourse markers are present (no obstante, ahora bien, de hecho) but not taught.
3. **Vocabulary repetition within stories is weak.** Most glossed items appear only once. The 3+ occurrences standard is met only by story-core nouns (sobre, reglamento, juramento, maqueta, papeleta). I did not add repetitions, to keep edits surgical. Cross-story recycling is decent (remitente, desconcertar, abatido, tambalearse, renuncia, suspicacia/suspicaz) and should be leveraged in flashcards. A few glossed items are below B2 (salvar, dividir, corregir, fracaso, alivio).
4. **Recurring motifs and names.** *Remedios* appears in three stories (02, 06, 10), *Anselmo* in two (03, 10), and *Quispe* in two (05, 07). An envelope is central in five of ten stories (01, 05, 07, 08, 10). b2-03 and b2-10 share a premise (an Aragonese village, a vote on renewable energy, reopening the school, a stubborn Anselmo). Per the constraints these are not changed, but I recommend renaming in a future pass and possibly re-themeing b2-10's campaign platforms.
5. **Quizzes.** Keys were correct apart from b2-05 Q4. Types were almost all detail and inference, with no vocab-in-context items. I added 5 (b2-01, 04, 05, 07, 09) and fixed one misquote. Some distractors remain too easy (e.g. "Que el balcón se derrumbó", "Renato lo había perdido jugando").
6. **Writing prompts.** They are clear and on-theme, but none had a model or starter, three named no target grammar, and one referenced a non-existent character. All 10 now end with a starter that uses the target structure.
7. **Translations.** The English is accurate and natural overall. Fixes were limited to conjecture (2), concession (1), the regional slang *gallego* (1) and three awkward phrasings.
8. **Typography.** Stories 06–10 used "— " (raya + space) in dialogue. This is normalised to "—".
