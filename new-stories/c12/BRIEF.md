## Quality bar (this is an advanced-level curriculum; be strict)
- Native-quality Spanish, regionally apt (voseo where the map says, ustedes in the Americas, vosotros only for Spain, regional lexicon used sparingly and glossed). No calques from English, no textbook stiffness, no repeated sentence frames. Rich syntax: long sentences with embedded clauses, varied connectors, free reported speech where the map asks; C2 should feel literary/journalistic/legal as mapped, with implicit meaning, irony, ellipsis.
- A real PLOT: a question the reader wants answered, rising tension, a resolution that rewards close reading (a clue planted early, paid off late). Characters have agency, professions, flaws, voice. No lecturing; the cultural/historical material emerges from the plot.
- Each target topic is DEMONSTRATED ≥3 natural times in the text (not forced), spread across the story; idioms in context with meaning inferable. Don't use structures beyond C2 or add exotic grammar that the grammar cards won't explain.
- Facts: every real-world fact in map 'facts' must be verified (use WebSearch/fetch). If a fact is marked 'verify' and you cannot confirm it, do NOT state it as fact: rephrase vaguely (e.g. 'hace más de un siglo') or invent a clearly fictional, non-checkable detail. Real institutions, places, laws, dates only if verified; invented: all characters, radio stations, businesses, newspapers, comparsas, documents. No fabricated quotes attributed to real persons. No stereotypes, exoticizing, or 'noble savage' tropes; communities are agents. Be respectful with sensitive history (slavery, discrimination, civil war exile). Mark minor language items you are not 100% sure of (Garífuna, Palenquero, Creole English, Guaraní, regional lexicon) in the notes so a native speaker can review.
- English translations: faithful, natural, not word-for-word.
- Vocab: 16–20 pairs, advanced useful words (not cognates), each wrapped in <b> exactly once in the Spanish lines; key semantic field per map; add a gloss form for verbs in infinitive.
- Quiz (stories file): 5 comprehension questions: detail, inference, attitude/implied meaning, vocabulary-in-context, one on the clue; 4 options, 1 correct (answer index 0–3), vary correct positions; distractors plausible; no trick questions.
- Grammar cards: 3–4 per story, topic strings EXACTLY as in map3 for that story; 'explain' in plain English (80–140 words, with the contrast with English or with B2-level structure where useful, and one common learner error), 3 examples QUOTED VERBATIM (tag-free, same punctuation) from the story text (verify programmatically that each example is a substring of the stripped text).
- Grammar quiz: exactly 8 questions per story = 4 core (each topic ≥1 core question) + 4 with "x": true; 4 options; one correct; answerable from the card; 'why' one sentence; cloze, correction and choose-the-register items; difficulty appropriate for C1/C2.
- concepts: {grammar, vocabulary, skills} in English, same style as B2 (grammar field lists the structures with quoted examples).
- Writing prompt (prompt): English task with a Spanish sentence starter, requires 2–3 of the story's target structures.
- Role-play (dialogues): one per story with the map's roleplay character: steps list of 5–6 steps; each step {say (Spanish), en, r:[[Spanish reply, English, nextIndex],...2–3 replies]}, last step {say,en,end:true}; replies differ in register/meaning; the conversation draws on the story's world, at the story's level and register (use usted/tú/vos appropriately). partners entry {name, g:'f'|'m', role} (name of the partner = a character IN the story). goals entry {goal, goalEs, opener, openerEn, scene} (open role-play with AI: a goal the learner must achieve in conversation, in C1/C2 register tasks like negotiating, persuading, correcting a misunderstanding).
- Also give per story: facts_used (list: fact → source URL) and review_notes (what a native speaker should check).
